Executive Summary

This report analyzes 301 SAP quality notifications (265 resolved, 36 open) from January 1 through April 15, 2026. Every notification in this dataset represents a preventable upstream failure—wrong data entered on a manifest, missing documents that should have been included before the load shipped, or generator records in SAP that were never corrected after prior errors.

The pattern is clear: these are not random mistakes. The same producers submit the same incorrect information load after load, and the same data fields (ERP numbers, phone numbers, generator attachments) fail repeatedly. 72% of all notifications stem from just two error types—ERP # and Generator Phone #—that could be eliminated entirely with a one-time master data cleanup. The fact that Boston Green Fuel has accumulated 55 notifications with the same recurring issues, and still has 16 open, indicates a systemic breakdown in both the producer's data entry practices and the upstream oversight that should be catching these errors before material arrives at our facilities.

This is not a receiving problem. Receiving is identifying the errors. The problem lives upstream: producers filling out manifests with wrong information, CSRs not verifying generator data before loads ship, and SAP master records that remain uncorrected despite repeat failures.

The Upstream Problem: Data Entry Failures & Missing Oversight

Every notification in this report traces back to one of two root causes: (1) the generator or their broker entered incorrect information on the manifest, or (2) the SAP master record for the generator was never updated after a prior error was identified. In either case, the failure happened before the load arrived at our facility.

The concentration of errors makes this unmistakable. ERP # errors (110 notifications) and Generator Phone # errors (108 notifications) together account for 72% of all issues. These are static data fields—a generator's EPA permit number and phone number do not change from load to load. When the same producer triggers the same ERP error on 30 separate shipments, it means the underlying SAP record was never corrected after the first error, and nobody upstream is validating the manifest against SAP before the load ships.

Generator attachments and missing documents (33 notifications) represent a different but equally preventable failure: the required paperwork was simply not included with the shipment. This is a packing and dispatch oversight at the generator site, compounded by a lack of pre-shipment checklist enforcement by the CSR or broker.

Financial Impact of Upstream Failures

Every paperwork notification requires approximately 1 hour of receiving and CSR staff time to investigate, contact the producer or customer, obtain the correct information, update SAP, and close the notification. At 301 notifications in 3.5 months, that's 301 hours of Tradebe staff time spent correcting mistakes that were made by producers and brokers before the load ever left the generator's site.

This is time that should be spent on production, not chasing down phone numbers and ERP entries that the producer should have gotten right the first time—or that should have been caught and corrected after the first failure.

The $302,536 in affected revenue represents loads sitting in the billing pipeline waiting for paperwork corrections. These are loads we've already received and processed—we're simply unable to bill for them until someone upstream provides the correct documentation.

Recommendations

1. Mandate SAP Master Data Correction for Top 10 Producers

  • 72% of notifications come from two data fields (ERP and phone) that are static and should never change between loads. The SAP generator record is wrong and nobody has fixed it. Require each CSR to audit and correct the SAP master record for their top 3 repeat offenders before accepting the next shipment.

  • This is a one-time fix that prevents hundreds of future notifications. The fact that Emerald submitted 28 loads with the wrong phone number before it was corrected is a process failure, not an anomaly.

2. Implement Pre-Shipment Manifest Validation

  • The CSR or their team should verify that the manifest matches SAP before the load ships. A 5-minute check upstream prevents 1+ hour of correction downstream. Start with the top 10 producers and expand. If the manifest ERP, phone, or generator ID doesn't match SAP, the load doesn't ship until it's corrected.

3. Clear the Boston Green Fuel Backlog Immediately

  • 55 notifications, 16 open, $14,880 affected, same errors repeating for 3.5 months. Megan DeClaire (Robert Cleary) must drive a corrective action call with BGF that results in a master data cleanup, SAP entry consolidation, and agreement on a pre-shipment checklist. This single action would clear 44% of the open backlog and 18.3% of all notifications.

4. Establish Re-pricing or Pre-qualification Gates for Worst Cost-to-Serve

  • GFL generates 37.4 notifications per $10K revenue. At an estimated $267/hr return on staff time, the cost of servicing their documentation errors may exceed the margin on their business. Consider: (a) adding a documentation surcharge for repeat offenders, (b) requiring a clean manifest as a condition of load acceptance, or (c) renegotiating pricing to reflect the true cost of service.

5. Open Notification Escalation Protocol

  • 22 of 36 open notifications are stuck at CSR Notified. Implement a 7-day escalation to the sales group lead. If a CSR has more than 5 open items past 7 days, the supervisor takes ownership. No notification should sit at CSR Notified for more than 10 business days.

6. Holistic Corrective Action for Dual Offenders

  • GFL (48 combined), US Industrial Tech (41), EnviroServe (32), and Axalta (37) fail on both paperwork and material quality. Schedule a single corrective action meeting per account that covers documentation, profile compliance, and material quality together. Two separate conversations with the same producer about two related problems is inefficient.

These are upstream problems with upstream solutions. Receiving is doing its job by catching the errors. The gap is between where the error is created (producer/manifest data entry) and where it's detected (receiving). Closing that gap—through master data correction, pre-shipment validation, and producer accountability—would reduce notification volume by an estimated 40–50% within one quarter.
